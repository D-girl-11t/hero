Hero Motor Control Simulation

Hero's vision and voice services run on Linux on the Cortex-A55. Its motors are driven by the Cortex-M33 running FreeRTOS, which generates the PWM and enforces a safety stop. The two cores talk over RPMsg: messages travel through shared DDR and the MU1 mailbox rings the doorbell. Drive the robot below and watch each command cross the chip.

RPMsg traffic

    Why the motors live on the M33

    • Steady PWM. A hardware timer on the M33 makes the pulses, so a busy Linux (NPU inference, speech recognition) can't stretch them.
    • A safety stop Linux can't break. If commands stop arriving for 500 ms, the M33 stops the motors on its own. Try Freeze Linux services.
    • Small, clear messages. Linux sends drv L R or stop over /dev/ttyRPMSG30; the M33 answers with what it applied.
    This page is an interactive model of Hero's motor-control design, running in your browser. It is not a live feed from the robot, and the camera view is drawn from the model, not captured. The collision guard reacts to obstacles the camera detects in front of Hero. Addresses come from the board's device tree: MU1 at 0x44230000, RPMsg vrings at 0xa4000000. Direction pins match Hero's wiring: IN1–IN4 on GPIO_IO02, 03, 04 and 14.
